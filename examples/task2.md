# Prompt Text
You should spend about 40 minutes on this task. Write at least 250 words.

Some people think that all university students should study whatever they like. Others believe that they should only be allowed to study subjects that will be useful in the future, such as those related to science and technology.

Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

# Essay Text
The two given views focus on personal preference and deployable utility respectively, both failing to strike a balance between all-round knowledge acquisition and pragmatics. In my opinion, students should be given the chance to discover and learn what they like, with students themselves taking the full responsibility of their lesson choices and honing competitiveness in future career.

The first view argues that students ought to learn whatever they like. This freedom provides a greater chance that students learn with bigger motivation and higher efficiency, because they are learning what they find interesting. This interest also leads to better academic performances. However, university studies also include boring but fundamentally useful parts, such as logic and ethics. These parts ensure that students develop mature behaviors and devite their lives to the greater good.

The second view claims that only useful subjects should be made accessible to university students. This argument dictates a strong dependency on future utility, which helps students to be more competitive in the job market. The problem is that it is insufficient to encourage students to master the foundational logic of these subjects, leaving students only capable of knowing how to think. The absence of the First Principle makes students vulnerable in tackling complex real world problems.

I reckon that universities should strike a balance between disciplines that are highly useful and disciplines that are personally favored by students. In the AI era, students who only know about mechanic knowledges are being replaced by AI-driven automation with an increasily fast speed. While those who master the foundational logic of human knowledge as well as utility-driven subjects related to STEM will be valued even more, since they are the architects who can leverage the super compute power of AI to create more values for the human society.

In conclusion, neither view has a balanced idea about what kind of subjects that university should permit students to learn. I argue that universities should provide both categories of lessons, while guiding students to become architects who learn not only what is useful but also what they like.
